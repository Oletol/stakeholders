What is a stakeholder?

Understanding who can affect a project and who can be affected by its outcomes.

Theory

Core concept

A stakeholder is a person, group or organisation that can affect a project or is affected by its outcomes.

Key point: a stakeholder is not necessarily a person who directly works on the project. A stakeholder may influence decisions, define requirements, regulate activity, provide resources, resist change or experience the consequences of project decisions.

Case 1 – AI Writing Assistant

A university plans to introduce an AI writing assistant for students. The system provides automated feedback on grammar, structure and academic style.

Task

  3. Explain how each stakeholder can affect the project or be affected by it.

Stakeholder identification

Using structured brainstorming and stakeholder mapping to avoid incomplete stakeholder lists.

Theory

Method 1 – Stakeholder brainstorming

  • Who will use the product or service?
  • Who makes decisions about the project?
  • Who provides resources?
  • Who can delay or block implementation?
  • Who evaluates the results?
  • Who will have to change current practices?
  • Who provides technical or organisational support?
  • Who regulates the project?
  • Who may benefit from the project?
  • Who may lose time, status, resources, autonomy or influence because of the project?

Influence and Impact Grid

Separating a stakeholder's ability to affect the project from the extent to which the project affects that stakeholder.

Influence

Influence is the degree to which a stakeholder can affect decisions, resources, implementation, acceptance or project outcomes.

Impact

Impact is the degree to which the project changes a stakeholder's work, responsibilities, access, opportunities, workload, experience or outcomes.

RACI for responsibility allocation

Defining who performs, owns, advises and receives information about project tasks.

R Responsible – performs the work.

A Accountable – owns the result and approves the final decision.

C Consulted – provides expertise, feedback or input.

I Informed – receives relevant information.

Integrated case – AI Supported Online Master's Programme

Combining all analytical tools in one digital education scenario.

A university is launching a new online master's programme using Moodle, automated assessment, AI assistants, video lectures, external industry experts and learning analytics.

  • Some teachers do not want to redesign their existing courses.
  • The IT department considers the implementation schedule unrealistic.
  • University management insists on a rapid launch.
  • Students are concerned about AI use and personal data.
  • Instructional designers report that some teachers ignore design requirements.
  • External experts want to use their own platforms and tools.
  • The university legal department requests changes to data processing procedures.
