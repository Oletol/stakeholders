Course Map

From the client's request and the needs analysis to an aligned course design

A course map is the central design document of your project. It shows the client how every element of the future course grows out of the needs analysis and serves the goal the client stated. Each level of the map breaks down into the next one.

Course title and the client's goalThe result the client expects, written in the client's words.e.g. Our engineers must take part in online meetings with partners without an interpreter.
Needs analysis conclusionsThe main findings of Components 1.1–1.4 that influence the design, together with the teaching approach, mode and scenario of the course.
Course goalsEducational goals that translate the client's goal into what learners will understand or know. Each goal is linked to the needs conclusions it responds to.Prompt: SS will understand / know …
Learning objectivesObservable, measurable results into which each goal is broken down.Prompt: SS will be able to …
AssessmentFor each objective, the assessment tools, their weight in the assessment plan, the rubric (criteria, descriptors, weight) and the types of materials designed for the assessment.
Learning activitiesFor each objective, the activities that prepare learners for the assessment and the types of materials designed for these activities.

Course Map Logic

Alignment between needs, goals, objectives, assessment and learning.

Key principles

Constructive alignment and backward design

The course map applies constructive alignment (Biggs & Tang): learning objectives, assessment and learning activities must point at the same result. It also follows backward design (Wiggins & McTighe): first define the results, then decide what evidence will show that learners have reached them, and only then plan the learning activities.

Client goal and course goals

The client formulates a goal in terms of the organisation's results. The designer translates it into course goals in educational terms. One client goal usually produces two to four course goals.

Goals and objectives

A course goal is broad (SS will understand / know …). A learning objective is specific and observable (SS will be able to …). A good objective names the action, the conditions and the expected level, for example the CEFR level. Use action verbs from Bloom's revised taxonomy: identify, summarise, compare, write, present, negotiate. Avoid understand, know and learn in objectives: they cannot be observed.

Traceability: every goal must be linked to at least one needs analysis conclusion, and every conclusion should be used by at least one goal. If a conclusion is not used, either the map misses something or the conclusion does not influence the design.
Assessment and materials

Assessment plan, rubrics and two types of materials

Assessment tools and the assessment plan

Each objective needs at least one assessment tool that shows whether learners have reached it. The weight of each tool in the course assessment plan is given in percent. All weights in the map add up to 100%.

Rubrics

A rubric makes the assessment transparent for learners, teachers and the client. It lists the criteria, a descriptor for each criterion and its weight. The criterion weights of one rubric add up to 100%. An automatically scored test may have one criterion.

Why the map has two columns of materials

Types of materials (assessment)Types of materials (learning)
Materials designed for the assessment tools: task sheets, test items, role cards, recording instructions, rubrics for learners. Materials designed for the learning activities: texts, audio and video, worksheets, interactive exercises, models and templates.

For each type of material, show which needs analysis conclusion it responds to, for example authentic recordings with accented speech because research and the survey identify listening as the main difficulty.

Team task

Build the course map for your client

  1. Enter the course title, the client, and the course goal exactly as the client stated it.
  2. Describe the teaching approach and mode, and the course scenario. Justify them with the needs analysis.
  3. Copy the main conclusions of your Comprehensive Needs Analysis that influence the course design. One conclusion per line.
  4. Formulate the course goals and link each goal to the needs conclusions it responds to.
  5. Break each goal down into learning objectives.
  6. For each objective, add the assessment tools: weight, rubric and types of materials.
  7. For each objective, add the learning activities and their types of materials.
  8. Check the course map table and the alignment check. Fix every warning before you show the map to the client.
  9. Export the course map as a PDF for your portfolio and for the meeting with the client.
